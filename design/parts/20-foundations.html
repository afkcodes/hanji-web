Foundations

01

A component names a token; it never names a value. Three papers, twelve inks and one set of springs are the whole contract an app has to satisfy. Depth comes from paper steps and hairlines, not shadows — they cost nothing to draw and survive both a white page and a true-black one.

    01.01

    Paper

    Three grounds, each with four surface steps and three inks. Steps move toward the light: on paper they whiten, on ink and charcoal they lift off black. Tap a sheet to use it.

    Steps
    bg → bgSoft → surface → surface2
    Paper
    blend(#f0ece3, white, .45 / .75 / 1)
    Ink
    blend(#000, white, .03 / .05 / .08)
    Charcoal
    blend(#111114, white, .04 / .07 / .11)
    Floors
    body ink 4.5 : 1 · muted ink 3 : 1
    Scrim
    black on every paper · onScrim #f4f2ef
    The linter's first real catch was hanji's own paper. Muted ink at a .42 blend measured 2.67 : 1 against its own 3 : 1 floor. It ships at .50 — 3.30 : 1, still clearly quiet. The ratios above are measured live.

    01.02

    Accent

    Twelve ramps, each a pair: a lighter stop for dark papers and a deeper one for light paper. An accent never arrives carrying ink chosen for another colour — its partners are derived. One accent per app, spent only where the user acts or has chosen.

    Status colours Proposed

    hanji ships one semantic hue, danger, and paints success with the accent. With a red accent that reads as an error, so this system adds success, warning and info — with no new hues: each borrows a ramp hanji already ships.

    Rule
    word + glyph + tint, never hue alone

    The accent is always a solid fill. Danger is only ever a tint with a glyph and a verb — never a solid red button — so “Save” and “Delete” can't be confused even in scarlet.

    01.08

    Depth

    There is no shadow scale. A raised thing is one paper step lighter with a hairline edge. Two exceptions: imagery gets one soft drop so a cover reads as an object, and — proposed here — anchored popovers get a lift, because nothing else separates them from the row they cover.
